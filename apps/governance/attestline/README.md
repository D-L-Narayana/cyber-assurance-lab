# Attestline — access-review decision engine

Attestline is an **educational prototype** of a periodic access-review (user-access certification) workbench. It takes a synthetic export of identities, resources and entitlements, works out *who should review what*, attaches explainable **risk hints** — separation-of-duties (SoD) conflicts, orphaned access of leavers, dormant and never-used grants, privileged entitlements, items nobody can review — and then enforces a small decision state machine (approve / revoke / delegate / campaign-owner routing) before exporting a certification record as JSON and CSV.

It is not connected to any identity provider or IAM system. Nothing it does changes a permission anywhere. It is not a compliance certification.

## What it demonstrates

| Workflow step | Where | Engine function |
|---|---|---|
| Validate a fixture (bounded, typed, dangling references) | "Load a different fixture" | `validateFixture`, `parseBoundedJson` |
| Route every entitlement to a reviewer (manager → resource owner → unrouted) | Reviewer chips, "Route unrouted items" | `buildCampaign`, `routeItem` |
| Compute risk hints and a deterministic risk score | Risk hints column, evidence drawer | `hintsFor`, `scoreOf` |
| Decide with guard rails: reason required when flagged, SoD override only with written rationale, no self-delegation, only the assigned reviewer may act | Evidence & decision drawer, keyboard shortcuts | `applyDecision` |
| All-or-nothing bulk decisions | Row checkboxes / Space key | `bulkDecision` |
| Completion by reviewer, SoD override count, unrouted count | Masthead | `completion` |
| Close the campaign: note ≥ 10 characters, refused while items are pending unless the closer acknowledges them (count recorded), then every decision/routing call refuses | "Close campaign" button → note dialog → closed banner | `closeCampaign` |
| Export a stable certification schema (`attestline.certification/v1`) with a SHA-256 digest over the canonical items/decisions/closing record, and a formula-safe CSV | Export JSON / Export CSV; digest in the masthead | `exportCertificationWithDigest`, `certificationDigest`, `toCsv` |


The bundled demo fixture (`src/fixtures/demo.json`) is generated by `node scripts/generate-fixture.mjs` with a fixed seed: 50 identities (6 managers, 41 staff/contractors, 3 service accounts), 11 resources, 147 entitlements and 3 SoD rules. It deliberately seeds two leavers who still hold privileged access, one suspended user, two SoD-violating identities, never-used privileged exports, a manager who no longer exists, and a legacy share whose owner left (so two items are *unrouted*).

## Algorithm

**Routing.** An entitlement goes to the identity's manager when the identity is `active` and the manager exists and is active; otherwise to the resource owner if active. A candidate who is the holder of the entitlement is always skipped (no self-review); `validateFixture` also rejects `managerId === id`. If no candidate is left the item is *unrouted* and carries a `no_reviewer` hint. Only the campaign owner can route an unrouted item (`routeItem`), and that action is recorded in the decision log as a delegation by the synthetic actor `campaign-owner`.

**Risk hints** (each with a plain-language `detail`):

- `sod_conflict` — the identity holds both privileges named in an SoD rule. Both sides are flagged and each points at its counterpart entitlement id.
- `orphan` — identity status is `leaver` or `suspended`.
- `dormant` — `lastUsed` is older than the configurable threshold (default 90 days; change it in the masthead, which rebuilds the campaign).
- `never_used` — `lastUsed` is null. Kept separate from dormant because "never exercised" is a different conversation than "stopped using".
- `privileged` — fixture marks the entitlement as privileged.
- `no_reviewer` — unrouted.
- `self_review` — the manager or resource owner *is* the holder of the entitlement, so the item was deliberately left unrouted for the campaign owner (nobody may certify their own access).

**Score** = sum of fixed hint weights (SoD 40, orphan 30, privileged 15, never used 12, dormant 10, no reviewer 8) + sensitivity weight (low 0 / moderate 5 / high 10) when at least one hint fires. Weights are opinionated defaults for ordering the queue, not a calibrated risk model; they are easy to find in `campaign.ts` and are covered by the determinism test.

**Decision rules.** Pending → approved/revoked (reversible while the campaign is open; every decision is appended with a sequence number). A reason is mandatory whenever an item has hints. Approving the second side of an SoD conflict while the first side is already approved requires an `sodOverride` with the matching rule id and a rationale of at least 10 characters — this is what makes "we accepted the risk" visible in the export instead of silently disappearing. Delegation needs a known **active** identity who is neither the actor nor the holder of the entitlement, and resets the item to pending under the new reviewer. `applyDecision` and `routeItem` both refuse any actor/target equal to the identity under review. Bulk decisions apply the same reason to every selected item and roll back entirely on the first rejection.

**Closing and the digest** (October 2026). `closeCampaign(campaign, { actor, at?, note, acknowledgePending? })` returns the same `{ ok, campaign } | { ok, error }` shape as every other transition. It requires an actor and a note of at least 10 characters, a valid closing date not before the campaign's as-of date (default: the as-of date), refuses a second close, and refuses while items are pending unless `acknowledgePending` is set — in which case the number of undecided items is written into the closing record. The result is additive: `campaign.closed = { at, by, note, pendingAtClose }`; items and decisions are untouched. Afterwards `applyDecision`, `routeItem` and `bulkDecision` return `Campaign is closed …`, and the UI disables the decision, bulk and routing controls and shows a closed banner. "Reset decisions" rebuilds the campaign and therefore starts a new, open one. `certificationDigest(json)` is SHA-256 (Web Crypto) over the canonical JSON — keys sorted at every depth — of `{ generatedFrom, asOf, config, items, decisions, closed }`; the summary, the disclaimer and the digest field itself are not covered. `exportCertificationWithDigest` adds `digest` (and `closed` when present) to the `attestline.certification/v1` JSON; the schema id and the CSV are unchanged, and `exportCertification` still exists for callers that do not need the digest. The digest makes an exported file tamper-*evident* (recompute and compare); it is not a signature and proves nothing about a real system.

## Architecture

```
src/engine/types.ts       domain types (Fixture, ReviewItem, DecisionRecord …)
src/engine/campaign.ts    pure functions: validateFixture, buildCampaign, applyDecision, routeItem, bulkDecision, closeCampaign, completion, exportCertification, canonicalJson, certificationDigest, exportCertificationWithDigest
src/engine/safe.ts        bounded JSON import, strict ISO dates, formula-safe CSV
src/ui/App.tsx            single-view React UI (no router; hash-free so it can be served from any sub-path)
src/fixtures/demo.json    seeded synthetic fixture (+ scripts/generate-fixture.mjs)
```

The engine has no React or DOM dependency and is exercised directly by Vitest. UI state (campaign, selection, acting reviewer) is React state in memory: **refresh resets everything** by design — there is no localStorage/sessionStorage/IndexedDB use, so the preview works in restricted browsers. Export before leaving.

## Tests

48 tests in 4 files (`Tests  48 passed (48)`):

- `src/engine/campaign.test.ts` — 28 tests: self-review guards (delegation to the holder, to a leaver, actor = holder, self-manager fixture, owner-of-own-resource rerouting), hint detection, routing, scoring determinism, state machine guards (reason, SoD override, self-delegation, wrong actor, reversal, atomic bulk), completion, export schema and CSV escaping, fixture validation including null/wrong-type rows and oversize arrays, campaign-owner routing.
- `src/engine/closure.test.ts` — 9 tests (October 2026): close refused with pending items unless acknowledged (count recorded), clean close with default date and untouched items, actor/note/date guards and double close, `applyDecision`/`bulkDecision`/`routeItem` refusing on a closed campaign while the same inputs succeed when open, `canonicalJson` key sorting, digest stability across key order, digest change on a decision or on the closing note, `exportCertificationWithDigest` keeping the v1 schema and CSV.
- `src/engine/safe.test.ts` — 10 tests: byte/depth/item bounds, `__proto__` rejection, array roots, calendar-invalid dates (`2026-02-30`), formula prefixes hidden behind whitespace/control characters.
- `src/fixtures/validate-demo.test.ts` — 1 test: the shipped fixture validates and contains every seeded finding.

## Data handling

- All data is synthetic: fictional names with surnames like *Example*, *Fixture*, *Placeholder*; service accounts use `.example`.
- Imported fixtures are parsed in the browser tab only, limited to 512 KB, 8 nesting levels, 5 000 values, 1 000 identities, 200 resources, 3 000 entitlements and 100 SoD rules. Malformed rows return bounded error lists instead of throwing.
- No network calls after the static bundle loads; `vercel.json` sets the lab-wide canonical headers (CSP `default-src 'none'`, `nosniff`, `DENY` framing, `no-referrer`, COOP/CORP, HSTS).
- CSV cells that could be read as spreadsheet formulas are prefixed with an apostrophe.
- The JSON export carries a SHA-256 `digest` computed in the browser with Web Crypto; nothing is sent anywhere to compute it.

## Limitations

- Hint weights and the dormancy threshold are heuristics, not a validated risk model; there is no peer-group or usage-pattern analysis.
- SoD rules are pairs of privilege names. Role-hierarchy inheritance, cross-resource transitive conflicts and attribute conditions are out of scope (see the companion Pathcaster project for graph reasoning).
- Reviewers are impersonated via a dropdown. There is no authentication; this is a single-user simulation of a multi-reviewer campaign.
- Closing is implemented as an in-memory freeze with a digest, not as a signed or persisted record: anyone holding the exported file can edit it and recompute the digest, and "Reset decisions" simply starts a new open campaign. Reminders, escalation and revocation fulfilment (actually removing access) are not implemented — the export is where a real IGA tool would start.
- Decisions are lost on refresh unless exported; there is intentionally no persistence.

## JD evidence (truthful framing)

This project gives concrete, runnable evidence for *access management and GRC reasoning*: SoD policy evaluation, dormant/orphan detection, reviewer routing, risk-accepted exceptions that stay visible, and an auditable export. It supports phrases like "built a test-first access-certification engine with SoD and dormant-access checks" — it does **not** support claims of SailPoint/Saviynt/ServiceNow experience, of running a real certification campaign, or of IAM administration.

## Design direction

Ink-and-stamp certification ledger: Big Shoulders Display (condensed uppercase) for the masthead and the rotated APPROVED/REVOKED stamps, IBM Plex Sans for prose, IBM Plex Mono for identifiers and dates. Bone-grey paper, white panels, ink rules; the only saturated colours are the stamp red, approve green and delegate amber, so colour always means a decision. Keyboard-first table with a persistent evidence drawer rather than cards or charts. Animations respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
