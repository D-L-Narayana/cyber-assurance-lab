# Project index

25 independent educational applications, verified locally on 1 October 2026. The 800 automated test cases include unit, integration, property and contrast checks where listed in each EVIDENCE.md; totals are not coverage percentages or security certifications.

| ID | Application | Track | Test cases | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---:|---|
| P1 | [Petitio](apps/privacy/petitio/README.md) | privacy | 68 | Privacy-operations teams handle individual rights requests (access, erasure, rectification, portability, opt-out of sale) against statutory clocks with extensions, holds, duplicates and multi-system reconciliation, and must be able to show why each decision was taken. |
| P2 | [Consentry](apps/privacy/consentry/README.md) | privacy | 51 | Teams need a single explainable answer to 'may this record be processed for this purpose for this subject right now?' across consent, legitimate interest, contract, children's rules and Global Privacy Control, with evidence of rule coverage. |
| P3 | [Tenure](apps/privacy/tenure/README.md) | privacy | 42 | Privacy engineers need a defensible map of where personal data lives, where it flows, who owns it and how long it is kept, with inconsistencies surfaced rather than hidden. |
| P4 | [Forethought](apps/privacy/forethought/README.md) | privacy | 37 | Privacy impact assessments are often prose documents whose risk ratings cannot be reproduced; teams need a repeatable rubric, mitigations that only count when implemented, and sign-off that is bound to the content it approved. |
| P5 | [Firstlight](apps/privacy/firstlight/README.md) | privacy | 36 | In the first hours of a personal-data incident, teams lose time to inconsistent timelines, unclear notification clocks, unexplained severity ratings and 'done' containment without evidence. |
| A1 | [Permit Matrix](apps/appsec/permitmatrix/README.md) | appsec | 28 | Object-, function- and property-level authorization bugs are found endpoint by endpoint; teams need the full role × object matrix with a negative case per cell. |
| A2 | [Provenance Gate](apps/appsec/provgate/README.md) | appsec | 22 | Release evidence is scattered and silently weak (stale threat model, self-approved review, wrong-commit scans, expired risk acceptances, altered artifacts); reviewers need one policy-driven gate that names each weak state. |
| A3 | [Assay Notebook](apps/appsec/assaynotebook/README.md) | appsec | 20 | Junior assessors need a safe place to learn evidence discipline: which observation supports which finding, why a severity was chosen, whether a fix was re-verified, and how to avoid duplicate write-ups. |
| A4 | [Seamline](apps/appsec/seamline/README.md) | appsec | 17 | Client/server (mobile-style) flaws are the same family every time — the server trusted a price, discount, points, role or object id the client sent — and are hard to see; assessors need the boundary made visible. |
| A5 | [Rootstock Review](apps/appsec/rootstock-review/README.md) | appsec | 21 | Dependency reports stop at '3 high'; maintainers need reachability hints, licence decisions, provenance flags and an upgrade plan that names the parent range blocking a fix — with unknowns marked as unknown. |
| G1 | [Tessera](apps/assurance/tessera/README.md) | assurance | 76 | Small teams need a defensible readiness view against NIST CSF 2.0 outcomes, not a green dashboard: which outcomes are actually evidenced right now, with what freshness, and why not. |
| G2 | [Packetsmith](apps/assurance/packetsmith/README.md) | assurance | 35 | Control assessors need repeatable, evidence-backed determinations and a packet that cannot be approved while incomplete or by its own author. |
| G3 | [Tierline](apps/assurance/tierline/README.md) | assurance | 33 | Vendor-risk teams need explainable segmentation: an inherent-risk score and tier whose fragility is visible, evidence requirements that expire, and a queue of concrete to-dos. |
| G4 | [Weft](apps/assurance/weft/README.md) | assurance | 36 | Reviewers must answer 'which evidence supports this assertion, and is it still the evidence that was signed?' and spot unexplained, duplicated or altered artifacts. |
| G5 | [Graceline](apps/assurance/graceline/README.md) | assurance | 34 | Security managers need risk-accepted policy exceptions that do not disappear: guarded approvals, time-boxed terms, visible expiry and escalation, and closure with evidence. |
| D1 | [Wireglass](apps/defense/wireglass/README.md) | defense | 20 | Junior analysts need to see how DNS/HTTP/SMTP protocol fields support triage, with rules whose false positives are visible and tunable rather than hidden. |
| D2 | [Peerline](apps/defense/peerline/README.md) | defense | 12 | Analysts need UEBA-style anomaly signals with readable reasons, measured precision/recall and a feedback loop, not an opaque score. |
| D3 | [Hashledger](apps/defense/hashledger/README.md) | defense | 17 | Endpoint teams need to reason about sensitive-file change and egress policy with verifiable evidence, without touching a real filesystem. |
| D4 | [Injectboard](apps/defense/injectboard/README.md) | defense | 11 | Teams need a repeatable, measurable incident-response tabletop with SLA clocks, role views and an after-action report, without malware or real systems. |
| D5 | [Ruleshadow](apps/defense/ruleshadow/README.md) | defense | 25 | Network reviewers need to find shadowed, conflicting, overbroad, expired and unused firewall/VPN rules and propose changes without touching a device. |
| DG4 | [Pathcaster](apps/governance/pathcaster/README.md) | governance | 30 | Reviewers cannot see which identities can reach a sensitive data asset through nested groups, roles and conditions, or why a path is blocked. |
| DG1 | [Attestline](apps/governance/attestline/README.md) | governance | 39 | Managers need evidence-backed approve/revoke decisions in periodic access reviews, with SoD conflicts, dormant and orphaned access made visible and risk acceptance recorded. |
| DG5 | [Outflow Register](apps/governance/outflow-register/README.md) | governance | 30 | Privacy/GRC teams lack a current view of what data is shared with which vendor, under which agreement, for how long, and whether obligations are evidenced. |
| DG3 | [Holdfast Ledger](apps/governance/holdfast-ledger/README.md) | governance | 26 | Data owners need to show what was deleted, what was kept under hold, and prove the record was not altered afterwards. |
| DG2 | [Labelsmith](apps/governance/labelsmith/README.md) | governance | 34 | Teams label data inconsistently and cannot explain why a field got a class or what handling it requires. |

Each directory contains setup instructions, limitations, an audit record and interview rehearsal material. All sample data is synthetic and no tool should be used for legal or compliance determinations.
